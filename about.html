About

Hi, I’m Jason.

I’m a photographer, filmmaker, and developer based in the Washington, DC area and the founder of A Digital Media Studio.

I started the studio with a simple idea: small businesses, organizations, and individuals deserve thoughtful creative and digital work without needing a different provider for every piece of the puzzle. I bring photography, video, web design, and development together so the camera and the code work toward the same goal.

For me it's about more than the finished product. It's about helping people bring their ideas to life in a way that feels true to them.

How I got here

Making things, for as long as I can remember

I grew up in Connecticut, and I've been in love with film for as long as I can remember. On my eleventh birthday my parents gave me a camcorder, and I wanted to understand everything about how movies were made. Before long I was building things out of whatever I could find, including a makeshift airplane out of an old refrigerator box.

When I was thirteen, my family moved to Wilmington, North Carolina. I dropped off a Polaroid and a submission form at the local Blockbuster, and that's how I booked my first commercial. It was improv-based, I had no idea what I was doing, and I loved it. Acting gave me an early love for storytelling and performance, but I was just as fascinated by everything happening behind the camera: how productions came together, how stories were shaped, and how I could make something of my own.

That curiosity took me into film production, photography, documentary work, and eventually web design and development. Along the way I studied acting and improv, worked in fast-moving production environments in New York, and spent time teaching and encouraging creativity in others. Each experience gave me a different view of the creative process and the people who make it happen.

Today I'm building on that foundation through my work and my graduate studies in Digital Media Design at Harvard Extension School.

How I like to work

I believe people are often more creative and capable than they give themselves credit for. Sometimes they just need someone to make the process a little less intimidating.

I enjoy demystifying the technical side of things: explaining the options, asking good questions, and making sure you have a real voice in the decisions that shape your project. Some people know exactly what they want and need someone to bring it to life. Others want to explore, experiment, and be part of the process. I'm comfortable with both.

Having spent time in front of the camera, behind it, and on the technical side of a project, I understand how different people experience the process. My goal is to make it collaborative, comfortable, and enjoyable, while delivering work that serves a real purpose.

I take the work seriously. That doesn't mean we can't have fun along the way.

Studio staff: my two Shih Tzus, Peanut and Buster, plus Juju, on loan from my girlfriend. They handle morale and quality control.
